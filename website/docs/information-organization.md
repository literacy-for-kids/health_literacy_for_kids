# Information Organization Skills for Body Systems and Habits

Health literacy includes body systems, symptoms, habits, routines, observations, questions, trusted adults, and privacy. Information Organization helps kids sort health ideas and questions — while respecting privacy and safety.

This page is the local doorway into the Literacy for Kids [Information Organization Toolkit](https://www.literacy-for-kids.com/docs/toolkits/information-organization), connected to the body-system understanding this curriculum builds.

## A few core ideas

- **Body systems have parts and relationships.** Diagrams make them visible.
- **Habit observations can be organized simply and privately.** Small notes, kept to yourself.
- **Questions for trusted adults are easier when written clearly.** What I noticed, what I wonder, who to ask.
- **Health information can be sensitive.** Privacy is part of the system.
- **Real health concerns need trusted adult or professional help.**

## When this shows up

- When learning body-system parts
- When comparing habits or routines
- When preparing a question for a trusted adult
- When noticing a pattern
- When using a private tracker
- When sorting symptoms, questions, and observations

## Tools that help

- **Body system diagram** — parts and arrows, not art.
- **Habit observation note** — small, private, dated.
- **Question list for a trusted adult** — written before the conversation.
- **Private information check** — what stays private, and who can help.
- **Observation / question / concern sort** — three piles before asking.

:::tip Information Organization Moment
Health information can be useful and private at the same time. If you are writing a question or observation, keep it simple: what I noticed, what I wonder, and who I should ask. Real health concerns need trusted adult support.
:::

:::caution Everyday organization skills, not medical advice
These are everyday information organization tools for learning about body systems, habits, and trusted questions, not medical advice. For real health concerns, involve a trusted adult or qualified professional. Health information can be private and should be handled carefully.
:::
