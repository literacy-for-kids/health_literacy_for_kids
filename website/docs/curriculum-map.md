# Curriculum Map

Use this map when you want the whole sequence on one page. The confidence labels here are teaching labels, not grades of importance.

---

| Week | Unit | Core concept | Skill practiced | Bio-Telemetry Log artifact | Check for understanding | Safety / privacy note | Science confidence label |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Week 1 | Control Loops | Homeostasis as self-regulation | Identify system alerts | System Alert Log | Define homeostasis, identify sensor and actuator, rename one symptom as an alert | Alerts are information; severe alerts need adult or medical help | Well-established + metaphor |
| Week 2 | Control Loops | Feedback loops stabilize variables | Diagram loops and trace cause-effect | Feedback Loop Diagram | Distinguish negative and positive feedback, map one loop | Loops are real biology, but living systems are noisy and multi-input | Well-established + metaphor |
| Week 3 | Control Loops | Baseline telemetry and recovery rate | Measure, record, compare to self | Baseline Telemetry Table | Take pulse, explain baseline, read a recovery pattern | Numbers are not grades; light activity only; no comparison | Well-established + educational measurement |
| Week 4 | Chemical Plant | Food labels measure energy input | Describe the digestive pipeline | Fuel Path Notes | Explain what Calories mean and where food goes next | No calorie counting; no moral labels for food | Well-established + simplified |
| Week 5 | Chemical Plant | Different inputs do different jobs | Compare fuel, structure, and helper molecules | Input Comparison Chart | Distinguish macronutrients from micronutrients | No good-food or bad-food language | Well-established + simplified |
| Week 6 | Chemical Plant | Glucose and insulin form a control loop | Trace variable, sensor, actuator, outcome | Glucose Loop Sketch | Explain a spike-crash cycle in plain language | One meal does not define health; no shame or food rules | Well-established + simplified |
| Week 7 | Chemical Plant | Input-output observation and correlation | Collect simple pattern data | Fuel and Energy Audit or Food-Free Audit | Distinguish input from output, form a hypothesis | No required food tracking; no public sharing of food logs | Educational observation |
| Week 8 | Threat Response | Immunity includes defense and regulation | Map layers of protection | Threat Barrier Notes | Name the firewall, innate response, and adaptive response | Symptoms can need adult help; immunity is not just attack | Well-established + metaphor |
| Week 9 | Threat Response | Fever and inflammation are system responses | Build a timeline from exposure to symptoms | Threat Timeline | Explain what fever is doing and when to pause for care | Fever having a purpose does not mean do not treat or seek care | Well-established + simplified |
| Week 10 | Threat Response | Illness episodes can be reconstructed | Analyze an illness case | Illness Timeline / Post-Mortem | Tell the difference between timeline, cause, and guess | Use fictionalized data if needed; illness histories are private | Well-established + educational reconstruction |
| Week 11 | Preventative Maintenance | Circadian timing coordinates many systems | Notice daily rhythms | Circadian Signal Tracker | Explain light timing, cortisol, and melatonin in simple language | Family and school schedules may conflict with biology; that is not the learner's fault | Well-established + simplified |
| Week 12 | Preventative Maintenance | Sleep is active maintenance | Track sleep and identify sleep stages | Sleep Tracker | Describe what sleep does for memory and repair | Sleep data is private; do not reduce sleep for experiments | Strong but simplified + active research |
| Week 13 | Preventative Maintenance | The microbiome is an ecosystem | Describe host-microbe exchange | Microbiome Observation Notes | Explain one thing microbes do and one limit of the analogy | No supplement trials; food variety is information, not a command | Strong but simplified + active research |
| Week 14 | Preventative Maintenance | Tissues adapt to load and recovery | Distinguish challenge from injury | Activity and Recovery Reflection | Explain wear, repair, adaptation, and pain boundaries | No intense new exercise routines; pain changes the plan | Well-established + simplified |
| Week 15 | Capstone | Protocol design turns curiosity into a study plan | Define variable, metric, and baseline | Protocol v1.0 | Write a question, metric, and safe plan | Forbidden variables include weight, calories, restriction, medication changes, and unsafe routines | Educational observation, not medical research |
| Week 16 | Capstone | Environment changes affect outcomes | Map constraints and choose feasible changes | Environment Audit | Identify support, friction, and low-cost changes | Some factors are outside the learner's control; no blame | Well-established + educational design |
| Week 17 | Capstone | Protocol testing requires consistent measurement | Track, troubleshoot, and interpret data | Daily Tracking Sheet and Midpoint Review | Explain adherence, missing data, and confounding factors | Missing data is not failure; privacy still applies | Educational observation |
| Week 18 | Capstone | Findings need careful interpretation | Present results, limits, and next steps | Protocol v2.0 and Final Reflection | Distinguish result from conclusion; share responsibly | Data can be anonymized, fictionalized, or partial | Educational observation |
| Optional 1 | Advanced Topics | Gut-brain signaling is real and still being mapped | Distinguish established findings from active research | Gut-Brain Notes | Explain one direct pathway and one research uncertainty | Do not prescribe probiotics or supplements | Emerging / active research |
| Optional 2 | Advanced Topics | Stress physiology has real hardware costs | Map activation and recovery | Stress Load Map | Explain acute stress, chronic stress, and recovery | Optional participation; fictional examples allowed; not therapy | Well-established in broad mechanism + individual variation |

## How to Use This Map

- Use it to preview the whole curriculum before you start.
- Use it mid-course if you need to slow down, skip an optional week, or revisit a concept.
- Use it with the [Facilitator Safety Guide](./facilitator-safety-guide.md), [Printable Templates](./printable-templates.md), [Facilitator Response Bank](./facilitator-response-bank.md), and [Science Confidence and Metaphor Guide](./science-confidence-and-metaphors.md).

Core weeks build the main sequence. Optional weeks deepen the science but are not required.
