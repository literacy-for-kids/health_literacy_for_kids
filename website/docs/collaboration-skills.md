# Collaboration Skills for Body Systems and Habits

Health literacy often involves trusted adults, family routines, asking questions, group activities, shared environments, and support. Collaboration helps kids understand that health habits and body learning often happen with other people — while real health concerns need adult or professional help.

This page is the local doorway into the Literacy for Kids [Collaboration Toolkit](https://www.literacy-for-kids.com/docs/toolkits/collaboration), connected to the body-system understanding this curriculum builds.

## A few core ideas

- **Health routines can involve shared support.** Families and classrooms often build habits together.
- **Trusted adults can help with questions and safety.** Asking is collaboration, not weakness.
- **Group habits need fair expectations.** Different bodies and situations need different things.
- **Privacy matters.** Support should respect what someone chooses to share.
- **Real health concerns need trusted adult or professional help.**

## When this shows up

- When planning a family or classroom routine
- When preparing a question for a trusted adult
- When doing group movement or health activities
- When discussing shared spaces like sleep, food, hygiene, or stress
- When someone needs support or privacy

## Tools that help

- **Shared routine goal** — what is the group trying to do together?
- **Role/support check** — what kind of help is actually helpful?
- **Privacy-respecting check-in** — asking without prying.
- **Trusted adult question card** — a clear question for a real concern.
- **Finish together reflection** — what support worked?

:::tip Collaboration Moment
A shared health routine works better when support is clear. Ask: "What are we trying to do together, and what kind of help is actually helpful?" Good support respects privacy and choice.
:::

:::caution Everyday collaboration skills, not medical advice
These are everyday collaboration tools for learning about body systems, habits, and support, not medical advice. For real health concerns, involve a trusted adult or qualified professional. Kids should not be expected to manage unsafe or serious health situations alone.
:::
