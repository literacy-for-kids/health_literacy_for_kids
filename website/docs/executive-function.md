# Executive Function Skills for Body Systems and Habits

Health literacy includes routines, observations, habits, questions, and maintenance. Executive function helps kids make health-related learning and everyday routines visible, small, and trackable.

This page is the local doorway into the Literacy for Kids [Executive Function Toolkit](https://www.literacy-for-kids.com/docs/toolkits/executive-function), connected to the body-system understanding this curriculum builds.

## A few core ideas

- **Habits need cues and small steps.** A cue helps a routine start without holding it in your head.
- **Body observations can be tracked simply and privately.** You never have to share private notes.
- **Routines are easier with checklists.** Visible steps reduce the "what next?" freeze.
- **Real health concerns need trusted adult or professional help.** Routines are not medical advice.

## When this shows up

- When building a sleep, food, movement, hygiene, or water routine
- When remembering health vocabulary
- When noticing a body pattern
- When preparing a question for a trusted adult
- When a tracker or journal feels too personal
- When a habit plan needs restarting

## Tools that help

- **Tiny habit first step** — one small, cued action.
- **Routine checklist** — the same short steps each time.
- **Private observation note** — kept to yourself.
- **Restart bookmark** — for picking a habit back up.
- **Trusted adult / professional help** — for real concerns.

:::tip Executive Function Moment
A health habit gets easier when the first step is tiny and visible. Instead of "be healthier," try "fill my water bottle after breakfast." Small cues help routines start.
:::

:::caution Everyday task skills, not medical advice
These are everyday planning and routine tools for learning body systems and habits, not medical advice. For real health concerns, involve a trusted adult or qualified professional. Kids should not be treated as lazy or broken when starting, organizing, or finishing is hard.
:::
