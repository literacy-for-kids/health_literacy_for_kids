# Health Systems Literacy Glossary

All the key words from our curriculum, explained in plain language. Terms are listed alphabetically. The **Introduced** column shows where each word first appears.

Some terms are biology terms. Some are engineering metaphors borrowed to help explain the biology. When a term is a metaphor, the definition says so.

---

| Term | Definition | Introduced |
|---|---|---|
| **Actuator** | A part of the body that takes action when a sensor reports a problem. Muscles, glands, and the heart are actuators because they sweat, shiver, pump faster, or release hormones to move the system back toward normal. | Week 1 |
| **Adaptive immunity** | The part of the immune system that learns. After fighting a new pathogen once, it builds a faster, more specific response for next time. | Week 8 |
| **Adherence** | How consistently a person followed the protocol they planned. In this curriculum, adherence is information, not a grade. | Week 17 |
| **Allostasis** | The process of adjusting internal systems to meet changing demands. Homeostasis is the stable range; allostasis is how the body shifts to keep functioning. | Week 18 |
| **Allostatic load** | The cumulative wear cost of repeated or chronic stress activation without enough recovery. Short stress can be useful; chronic overload is expensive to the system. | Optional 2 |
| **Antibody** | A protein the immune system creates that locks onto a specific target. Antibodies help the system recognize and tag threats more efficiently. | Week 8 |
| **Antigen** | A molecule or structure that immune cells or antibodies can recognize as foreign. | Week 8 |
| **ATP** | The body's main energy currency. Cells convert fuel from food into ATP, and ATP powers nearly every action a cell performs. | Week 4 |
| **Bandwidth** | A way of describing the brain's working capacity at a given moment. Low sleep, low fuel, illness, or high stress can all reduce bandwidth. | Week 12 |
| **Baseline** | A starting measurement of a system in its usual state. Without a baseline, it is hard to tell whether something has changed. | Week 3 |
| **Bio-Telemetry Log** | The private journal used throughout this curriculum. Students record measurements, observations, and system alerts from their own bodies over 18 weeks, and sharing is optional. | Week 1 |
| **Body data** | Measurements or observations about your own body, such as sleep times, heart rate, energy ratings, or notes about system alerts. It belongs to the learner and should be handled privately. | Week 15 |
| **Boot sequence** | A borrowed computing metaphor for the body's morning startup routine. Cortisol rises, body temperature climbs, and the system transitions from sleep mode to active operation. | Week 11 |
| **Carbohydrate** | A macronutrient that breaks down into glucose for energy. Some carbohydrates are processed quickly and some more slowly, depending on their structure and context. | Week 5 |
| **Causation** | When one thing actually helps produce another thing. Correlation alone does not prove causation. | Week 15 |
| **Circadian rhythm** | The body's internal 24-hour clock. It helps coordinate sleep, alertness, hormone release, body temperature, and many other functions. Light is its main time signal. | Week 11 |
| **Complex carbohydrate** | A carbohydrate made of longer, more complex chains that usually take more time to break down, such as beans, oats, and many whole grains. | Week 6 |
| **Confounding factor** | Something besides the target variable that can influence the result and make a pattern harder to interpret. | Week 17 |
| **Context** | Background information that helps explain a measurement, such as being sick, traveling, having a late night, or dealing with an unusual event. | Week 17 |
| **Control loop** | A self-correcting system. A sensor measures a variable, compares it to a target, and triggers an actuator if the variable drifts. The body runs many of these. | Week 1 |
| **Correlation** | When two things change together, even if one did not cause the other. | Week 15 |
| **Cortisol** | A steroid hormone released by the adrenal cortex. It helps with alertness and the stress response in the short term, but staying elevated for long periods can be costly to the body. | Week 11 |
| **Cytokine** | A signaling molecule used by immune cells to coordinate inflammation and other parts of the immune response. | Week 9 |
| **Defragmentation** | A borrowed computing metaphor for one part of what sleep does. During sleep, the brain consolidates memories and clears some metabolic waste, but sleep is more complex than a literal disk cleanup. | Week 12 |
| **Fat** | A macronutrient that stores a lot of energy and also helps build cell membranes and other structures. | Week 5 |
| **Feedback loop** | Another name for a control loop. Most body feedback loops are negative feedback loops that push the system back toward normal. | Week 2 |
| **Fever** | A deliberate elevation of body temperature during an immune response. Fever can make the environment harder for some pathogens, but a fever still needs normal safety judgment and care. | Week 9 |
| **Firewall** | A borrowed cybersecurity metaphor for the body's physical barriers such as skin, mucus, stomach acid, and tears. These barriers are the first line of defense against pathogens. | Week 8 |
| **Firmware** | A borrowed engineering metaphor for built-in biological programs the body runs without conscious thought, such as circadian rhythms, reflexes, and parts of the immune response. | Week 1 |
| **Fuel quality** | A non-moral way of talking about what a food input does in the system. It asks about energy, structure, timing, fiber, and helper molecules rather than labeling food or people as good or bad. | Week 5 |
| **GABA** | Gamma-aminobutyric acid, an inhibitory neurotransmitter that helps quiet neural activity. | Optional 1 |
| **Glucagon** | A hormone released by the pancreas when blood glucose gets too low. It raises blood glucose and works as a counter-signal to insulin. | Week 6 |
| **Glucose** | A simple sugar that many cells use directly for energy. Blood glucose is one of the body's most tightly regulated variables. | Week 6 |
| **Glymphatic system** | The brain's waste-clearance system. During sleep, fluid movement through brain tissue and changes in the interstitial space between brain cells help clear metabolic byproducts, and scientists are still studying the exact details. | Week 12 |
| **Hardware** | A borrowed engineering metaphor for the body's physical structures such as bones, organs, muscles, skin, and blood vessels. | Week 1 |
| **Homeostasis** | The body's ability to keep its internal environment within workable ranges even when conditions outside change. Temperature, blood sugar, blood pressure, and fluid balance are common examples. | Week 1 |
| **Hormone** | A chemical messenger released by one part of the body and carried to other tissues, where it changes what cells do. Some hormones are proteins; others, like cortisol and melatonin, are not. | Week 6 |
| **HPA axis** | Short for hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis. It is a communication pathway involved in the stress response that helps tell the adrenal cortex when to release cortisol. | Optional 2 |
| **Immune system** | The body's defense network. It includes physical barriers, fast general responses, and slower learned responses that remember specific threats. | Week 8 |
| **Inflammation** | A controlled damage-control response. Blood vessels widen, immune signals rise, and the area may become warm, swollen, or sore. Useful in the short term, expensive over time. | Week 9 |
| **Innate immunity** | The fast part of the immune system that responds to many threats without having to learn them first. | Week 8 |
| **Insulin** | A hormone released by the pancreas when blood glucose rises. It helps cells take in glucose and brings blood sugar back toward baseline. | Week 6 |
| **Intervention** | The one intentional change made during a protocol to see whether the metric changes. | Week 15 |
| **Interstitial space** | The fluid-filled space between cells. In sleep research, changes in the interstitial space between brain cells are part of how waste clearance is studied. | Week 12 |
| **Kilocalorie / food Calorie** | On food labels, a Calorie with a capital C means kilocalorie. It is a unit of energy, not a moral judgment and not a rule by itself for what someone should eat. | Week 4 |
| **Macronutrient** | One of the three big nutrient categories: carbohydrates, fats, and proteins. They supply energy, building material, or both. | Week 5 |
| **Maintenance cycle** | A period of active repair, cleanup, and reorganization. Sleep is the body's main maintenance cycle. | Week 12 |
| **Melatonin** | A small hormone released by the pineal gland in response to darkness. It helps signal that it is time to sleep; it is not a guarantee that sleep will happen instantly. | Week 11 |
| **Metabolism** | The total set of chemical reactions that convert fuel into energy, structure, storage, and waste products. | Week 4 |
| **Metaphor** | A borrowed comparison used to make a complex idea easier to picture. In this curriculum, words like firewall, boot sequence, and hardware are metaphors, not literal body parts. | Week 1 |
| **Micronutrient** | Vitamins and minerals needed in small amounts to help enzymes, signaling pathways, and other chemical reactions work. They are not fuel, but the system depends on them. | Week 5 |
| **Microbiome** | The trillions of microbes that live in and on the body, especially in the gut. They help digest food and produce compounds that can influence other body systems. | Week 13 |
| **Negative feedback loop** | A control loop that pushes a variable back toward a target when it drifts away. Most body regulation works this way. | Week 2 |
| **Neurotransmitter** | A chemical messenger used mostly by neurons to pass signals across synapses. Neurotransmitters usually work over short distances. | Week 13 |
| **Null result** | A finding where the data does not show a clear change or effect. Null results still matter because they answer the question honestly. | Week 18 |
| **Pathogen** | A foreign organism or particle, such as a virus, bacterium, fungus, or parasite, that can cause illness if it gets past the body's barriers and defenses. | Week 8 |
| **Positive feedback loop** | A control loop that amplifies a signal instead of damping it. Useful in specific situations, but risky if it runs unchecked. | Week 2 |
| **Privacy** | The rule that personal body data belongs to the learner and should only be shared with consent. | Week 1 |
| **Protein** | A macronutrient and structural material. Muscles, enzymes, antibodies, and some hormones are made of protein. | Week 5 |
| **Protocol** | The written plan for what variable you are tracking, how you will measure it, when you will measure it, and what change, if any, you are testing. | Week 15 |
| **Recovery rate** | How quickly the body returns toward baseline after a stress such as exercise. It is one useful piece of personal telemetry. | Week 3 |
| **REM sleep** | Rapid Eye Movement sleep. This sleep stage is associated with vivid dreaming and important memory and emotional processing. | Week 12 |
| **Sensor** | A part of the body that measures a variable. Thermoreceptors measure temperature, baroreceptors measure blood pressure, and pancreas cells help sense blood glucose. | Week 1 |
| **Serotonin** | A signaling molecule often discussed as both a neurotransmitter and a body-wide messenger. Much of the body's serotonin is made in the gut and acts locally there, and its relationship to mood is indirect and still being studied. | Week 13 |
| **Simple carbohydrate** | A carbohydrate made of shorter chains that breaks down quickly, such as table sugar or many refined starches. It can produce a faster rise in blood glucose. | Week 6 |
| **Software** | A borrowed engineering metaphor for learned behaviors, habits, and trained skills that can change over time. | Week 1 |
| **Symbiotic** | A relationship between two different organisms that helps both. The human-microbiome relationship is often described as symbiotic. | Week 13 |
| **System alert** | A signal from the body that something has drifted outside a workable range. Hunger, thirst, fatigue, pain, and fever can all function as system alerts. | Week 1 |
| **Telemetry** | Measurements of a system's internal state. Heart rate, respiration rate, body temperature, blood pressure, and blood oxygen are examples of telemetry. | Week 3 |
| **Throughput** | The rate at which a system processes material or energy. Metabolic throughput is the rate at which the body processes fuel. | Week 4 |
| **Trauma-informed** | A way of teaching that assumes some learners may have stressful or traumatic experiences and avoids forced disclosure, shame, or unsafe participation. | Optional 2 |
| **Vagus nerve** | A major nerve that connects the brain to the gut, lungs, heart, and other organs. It is one important pathway in gut-brain and parasympathetic signaling. | Optional 1 |
| **Variable** | The thing you choose to observe or measure in a protocol. | Week 15 |
