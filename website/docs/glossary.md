# Health Systems Literacy Glossary

All the key words from our curriculum, explained in plain language. Terms are listed alphabetically. The **Introduced** column shows where each word first appears.

---

| Term | Definition | Introduced |
|---|---|---|
| **Actuator** | A part of the body that takes action when a sensor reports a problem. Muscles, glands, and the heart are actuators — they sweat, shiver, pump faster, or release hormones to bring the system back to normal. | Week 1 |
| **Adaptive immunity** | The part of the immune system that learns. After fighting a new pathogen once, it writes "virus definitions" (antibodies) so it can recognize and shut down the same attacker faster next time. | Week 8 |
| **Allostatic load** | The cumulative wear-and-tear cost of running the body in high-alert mode for long periods. Short stress is a feature; chronic stress is a hardware expense. | Optional 2 |
| **Antibody** | A protein the immune system creates that locks onto one specific pathogen. Think of antibodies as virus definitions — once written, the system can recognize that exact threat instantly. | Week 8 |
| **ATP** | The body's main energy currency. Cells convert fuel from food into ATP molecules, and ATP is what powers nearly every action a cell performs. | Week 4 |
| **Bandwidth** | A way of describing the brain's cognitive capacity at any given moment. Low sleep, low fuel, or high stress all reduce bandwidth. | Week 12 |
| **Baseline** | A starting measurement of a system in its normal state. Without a baseline, you cannot tell whether something has changed. | Week 3 |
| **Bio-Telemetry Log** | The journal kept throughout this curriculum. Students record measurements, observations, and system alerts from their own bodies over 18 weeks. | Week 1 |
| **Boot sequence** | The morning startup routine. Cortisol rises, body temperature climbs, and the system transitions from sleep mode to active operation. | Week 11 |
| **Carbohydrate** | A fuel type that breaks down into glucose for quick energy. Simple carbohydrates break down fast and cause sharp glucose spikes; complex carbohydrates break down slowly and release energy gradually. | Week 5 |
| **Circadian rhythm** | The body's internal 24-hour clock. It governs sleep, alertness, hormone release, body temperature, and many other functions. Light is its primary time signal. | Week 11 |
| **Complex carbohydrate** | A carbohydrate made of long, branching sugar chains that take time to break down — like whole grains, beans, and most vegetables. The result is steady fuel release. | Week 6 |
| **Control loop** | A self-correcting system. A sensor measures a variable, compares it to a target, and triggers an actuator if the variable drifts. The body runs hundreds of these. | Week 1 |
| **Cortisol** | A hormone released by the adrenal glands. Useful in short bursts (boot sequence, threat response) but costly to the system when elevated for long periods. | Optional 2 |
| **Defragmentation** | A computing term we borrow for sleep. During sleep, the brain reorganizes data (memory consolidation) and clears out metabolic waste. | Week 12 |
| **Fat** | A fuel type stored as long-term reserve. Fat carries more than twice the energy per gram of carbohydrates or proteins and is also a structural material in cell membranes. | Week 5 |
| **Feedback loop** | Another name for a control loop. Most are negative feedback loops (they push the system back toward normal). A few are positive feedback loops (they amplify the signal — useful in specific situations). | Week 2 |
| **Fever** | A deliberate elevation of body temperature by the immune system. Most pathogens replicate worse at higher temperatures, so fever is the system raising the difficulty setting on the invader. | Week 9 |
| **Firewall** | The body's physical barriers — skin, mucus, stomach acid, tears. The first line of defense against pathogens. | Week 8 |
| **Firmware** | Biological programs the body runs without conscious thought — the circadian rhythm, the immune response, reflexes. Not learned, not chosen; built in. | Week 1 |
| **Fuel quality** | A way of talking about food without "good food" / "bad food" judgment. Fuel quality is about what the fuel does in the system — how fast it breaks down, what structural materials it provides, what micronutrient lubricants it carries. | Week 5 |
| **Glucose** | The simple sugar that most cells use directly for energy. Blood glucose is one of the most tightly regulated variables in the body. | Week 6 |
| **Glymphatic system** | The brain's waste-clearance system. It activates during sleep and flushes out metabolic byproducts that accumulate during the day. | Week 12 |
| **Hardware** | The physical structures of the body — bones, organs, muscles, skin, blood vessels. What you can touch (or could touch, if you were inside). | Week 1 |
| **Homeostasis** | The body's ability to maintain a stable internal environment despite changes outside. Temperature, blood sugar, blood pressure, fluid balance — all are kept within tight ranges by control loops. | Week 1 |
| **HPA axis** | The hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis. A communication pathway that activates during stress and tells the adrenal glands to release cortisol. | Optional 2 |
| **Immune system** | The body's defense network. Includes physical barriers, innate cells that respond to anything foreign, and adaptive cells that learn specific threats. | Week 8 |
| **Inflammation** | A controlled damage-control response. Blood vessels widen, immune cells flood in, and the area becomes warm and swollen. Useful in short bursts; expensive over time. | Week 9 |
| **Innate immunity** | The part of the immune system that responds to any foreign threat without needing to learn it first. Fast but not specific. | Week 8 |
| **Insulin** | A hormone released by the pancreas when blood glucose rises. Insulin is the actuator that tells cells to absorb glucose, lowering blood sugar back to baseline. | Week 6 |
| **Macronutrient** | The three big fuel categories: carbohydrates, fats, and proteins. The body needs all three in different proportions. | Week 5 |
| **Maintenance cycle** | A period of active repair and reorganization. Sleep is the body's main maintenance cycle. | Week 12 |
| **Melatonin** | The hormone that signals "it is time to sleep." Released by the pineal gland in response to darkness. | Week 11 |
| **Metabolism** | The total set of chemical reactions that convert fuel into energy and structural materials. Often described as the body's throughput rate. | Week 4 |
| **Micronutrient** | Vitamins and minerals. Needed in small amounts to keep enzymes and chemical reactions working. The lubricants of the chemical plant. | Week 5 |
| **Microbiome** | The trillions of microbes (mostly bacteria) that live in and on the body, especially in the gut. A symbiotic co-processor that helps digest food and produces compounds the body uses. | Week 13 |
| **Negative feedback loop** | A control loop that pushes a variable back toward a target value when it drifts. Most of the body's regulatory loops work this way. | Week 2 |
| **Pathogen** | A foreign organism (virus, bacterium, fungus, parasite) that can cause illness if it gets past the firewall. | Week 8 |
| **Positive feedback loop** | A control loop that amplifies a signal instead of dampening it. Useful in specific situations — like blood clotting, where you want the clot to grow fast — but dangerous if it runs unchecked. | Week 2 |
| **Protein** | A fuel type, but more importantly a structural material. Muscles, enzymes, antibodies, and many hormones are made of protein. | Week 5 |
| **Recovery rate** | How quickly the body returns to baseline after a stress (like exercise). A core piece of personal telemetry. | Week 3 |
| **REM sleep** | "Rapid Eye Movement" sleep. The stage of sleep where most dreaming happens. The brain is highly active during REM, consolidating memories and processing experiences. | Week 12 |
| **Sensor** | A part of the body that measures a variable. Thermoreceptors measure temperature. Baroreceptors measure blood pressure. Glucose sensors in the pancreas measure blood sugar. | Week 1 |
| **Simple carbohydrate** | A carbohydrate made of short sugar chains that break down very fast — like table sugar, candy, or white flour. Produces a sharp glucose spike. | Week 6 |
| **Software** | Learned behaviors, habits, and trained skills. Software changes over time based on experience. | Week 1 |
| **Symbiotic** | A relationship between two different organisms that helps both. The human-microbiome relationship is symbiotic. | Week 13 |
| **System alert** | A signal from the body that something has drifted outside normal range. Hunger, thirst, fatigue, pain, and fever are all system alerts. | Week 1 |
| **Telemetry** | Measurements of a system's internal state. Heart rate, respiration rate, body temperature, blood pressure, and blood oxygen are common telemetry signals. | Week 3 |
| **Throughput** | The rate at which a system processes material. Metabolic throughput is the rate at which the body processes fuel. | Week 4 |
| **Vagus nerve** | A major nerve that connects the brain to the gut, lungs, heart, and other organs. The main communication channel for the gut-brain axis. | Optional 1 |
